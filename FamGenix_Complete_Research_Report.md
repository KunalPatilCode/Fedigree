# 1. Summary in one paragraph

FamGenix is a health technology software company. It makes tools for recording family health history, drawing family-tree charts called pedigrees, and assessing inherited disease risk. Its software helps hospitals and genetic counsellors identify people who may have a higher risk of conditions such as certain cancers or heart disease. It can also help healthcare professionals decide who may need genetic counselling or genetic testing. The software supports risk assessment; it does not diagnose a disease by itself.

# 2. What are a pedigree and hereditary risk?

A pedigree chart is a medical family tree. It shows family relationships and may include each relative’s diseases, age, and genetic test results. Doctors use it to check whether a disease appears across several generations.

Hereditary risk means that a person may inherit a genetic change that increases their chance of developing a disease. For example, certain changes in the BRCA1 and BRCA2 genes can increase the risk of breast and ovarian cancer. Having a risk-related gene change does not always mean the person will develop the disease.

A common problem is that family history is often collected on paper, from memory, or with missing information. Doctors may also have limited time. As a result, some people who could benefit from further assessment may not be identified. FamGenix aims to make this process digital, structured, and more automatic.

# 5. Products and solutions

## A. Provider Portal: pedigree drawing for clinicians

The Provider Portal helps clinicians create medical family trees quickly, including complex family relationships. The report says it can represent relationships such as marriage between relatives, same-sex relationships, and donor or surrogate relationships.

Main functions include:

- Uses structured medical terms and coding systems, including HPO, OMIM, CHV, NCI, and HGNC for gene names. This can make information easier to organise and exchange with other systems.

- Tracks genetic test results. It includes tests from major laboratories and can support custom tests.

- Lets clinicians control what appears on the chart, including symbols, partners, selected conditions, notes, and ancestry details.

- Supports custom searches, filters, reports, and export to different formats.

- Allows documents to be uploaded for each patient and pedigrees to be shared with patients.

- Can import existing pedigrees from other software, such as Progeny.

- Can integrate the pedigree into a hospital electronic health record (EHR), according to the report.

- The report lists regional data storage options for the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom/Europe, and Australia.

## B. Patient app and questionnaires

The patient app works on mobile devices and in a web browser. Before a clinical visit, a patient answers a questionnaire. The answers can be used to create a family pedigree automatically.

- Hospitals can create their own questionnaires and lists of conditions.

- The report lists English, Spanish, French, German, and Dutch as supported languages.

- Patients can maintain an ongoing digital family health record.

- Hospitals can use population screening workflows for conditions such as cancer, cardiovascular disease, and neurological disease.

- FamShare allows patients to invite family members. Each member controls their own record and can turn sharing on or off. This may help relatives learn about possible risks and pursue appropriate screening, sometimes called cascade screening.

## C. Genetic risk assessment models

The report says that FamGenix can run several risk models at once, using the information already entered. The listed models and their intended uses are:

| **Model**                                             | **Purpose**                                                                                                            |
|-------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Tyrer-Cuzick v8 (IBIS)                                | Breast cancer risk.                                                                                                    |
| BOADICEA v6 (CanRisk)                                 | Breast and ovarian cancer risk.                                                                                        |
| BayesMendel models: BRCAPRO, MMRpro, PancPRO, MelaPRO | Different cancer risks, including breast/ovarian, colorectal, pancreatic cancer, and melanoma, depending on the model. |
| Gail                                                  | Breast cancer risk.                                                                                                    |
| Claus                                                 | Breast cancer risk.                                                                                                    |
| QRISK3                                                | Cardiovascular risk.                                                                                                   |

The source report says the platform can show 5-year, 10-year, and lifetime risk estimates where supported by a model. It also says that it can estimate the probability of certain gene mutations, including BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, ATM, and CHEK2.

The software can show alerts when required information is missing. It can also check referral criteria from guidelines such as NCCN and recommendations from ACMG/NSGC and the American Society of Breast Surgeons. It can explain why a person appears to meet a referral criterion.

## D. White-label and business integration

FamGenix can be embedded in the software used by a hospital or an electronic health record vendor. In a white-label setup, the feature can appear under the healthcare organisation’s branding.

- The pedigree module can support single sign-on (SSO), so users may access it through the host system.

- Patient intake forms can use the healthcare organisation’s branding.

- The Risk Server API allows another system to send data to FamGenix and receive risk-model results.

- The source report says the patient’s data can remain within the customer’s system for this type of integration.

- Custom projects are quoted separately.

# 7. Security and privacy

- The source report says that FamGenix uses servers described as HIPAA- and GDPR-compliant.

- Data is encrypted while stored and while being sent between systems.

- The report says the company completed a SOC 2 Type 1 examination in April 2026.

- The report says data is stored in a region close to the user’s region.

- FamGenix says it does not sell data. The source report says that if de-identified data were offered for research in the future, patients would be notified and would have to opt in.

- A patient can grant a clinician access through a Clinician ID and can revoke that access later.

## Possible market gap in India

- The source report says genetic counselling in oncology is still developing in India and that there are not many counsellors.

- Guidelines may recommend collecting a three-generation family history, but complete histories can be difficult to obtain in practice.

- Consanguineous marriages and conditions such as thalassemia make access to appropriate genetic counselling important in some communities.

- These observations suggest that a tool like FamGenix may be useful in India, but demand and local clinical requirements would need further study.

## Other global tools mentioned

- Progeny: established pedigree software.

- PhenoTips: pedigree and phenotype tools with EHR integration options.

- TrakGene: another option for genetic counsellors.

- CanRisk (BOADICEA): a web-based tool for breast and ovarian cancer risk, with research/academic use options.

- Creately: basic diagram or genogram drawing, not a full clinical risk-model platform.

The source report notes that many global tools use US or UK guidelines such as NCCN and ACMG. Models that are valid for Indian populations, and guidance aligned with Indian clinical practice, are separate issues that need attention.

# 11. Strengths and limitations

## Strengths reported

- The founders have experience with pedigree software, including Progeny.

- Several established risk models can be accessed in one platform.

- The software can automatically check some guideline-based referral criteria.

- It offers EHR integration and white-label embedding.

- The patient app is available without charge at the free level, and FamShare supports controlled family sharing.

- The company reports security and privacy measures, including encryption and a SOC 2 Type 1 examination.

- The source report lists well-known healthcare organisations among its customers or customer-logo references.

## Limitations and items to check

- The source report estimates the company has about 10 employees and about US\$1.27 million in funding; these figures come from third-party sources.

- The source report did not find independent published evidence of patient outcomes on the company website.

- Premium pricing is not public.

- The report says hereditary cancer risk assessment in the patient app is available only to users in the United States and Canada.

- The software estimates risk; it does not make a diagnosis. Final decisions belong to qualified clinicians or genetic counsellors.

- The source report notes inconsistent headquarters information on the website (Florida versus Fargo).

- The report noticed the names “MLH2” and “MLH6” in a mutation-probability list and suggested they may be typos for MSH2 and MSH6. This was the report author’s inference and should be checked with the company; it should not be assumed to be a confirmed error.

- The source report did not find information showing that the models are validated specifically for Indian populations.

- The report was based on public company information and some secondary sources; independent user reviews were not reviewed.
